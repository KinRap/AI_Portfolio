I design and build multi-agent AI systems that hold up to scrutiny - with deterministic audit layers instead of guesswork - and I can explain exactly why they work to the people who need to trust them. Technical depth and clear communication rarely live in the same person. They do here.

Why AI

AI caught me during my technical-communication studies, on a project about using it with the tools we had at the time.

Building that project, I ran straight into both the walls and the open doors - the real limits of the tools, and possibilities I hadn't expected. That was the moment it stopped being a subject I was studying and became something I couldn't put down. I got pulled in, and I started learning how to use AI properly - not as a gimmick, but as part of how I work every day. I can't picture working without it now.

What keeps me here was never blind enthusiasm. My analytical mind refuses to take AI on faith: it argues with the output, verifies it, pushes back - and at the same time goes looking for the non-obvious connections, the uses nobody wrote into the manual. That tension between skepticism and experimentation is the whole point, and it's why my systems carry audit layers instead of assumptions.

Every course and certification pulled me deeper and gave me more room to experiment, until the experiments turned into projects that actually worked - and changed how I think about agents entirely. I keep investing in myself and testing the edges, but I build for the other side of the screen too: practical systems that make real work easier for real people. I know this field never stands still, and that's exactly what I like about it.

The research years - a doctorate and two master's - taught me to be skeptical and to verify before I believe. In AI, that isn't baggage, it's the edge.

How I work

Principles, not slogans

The way I build AI comes straight from the research and teaching years - and it shows in the systems.

01

Measured, not imagined

If a system claims to be reliable, I want the number. My pipelines carry deterministic audit layers that score each step on recall and precision - so "it works" is a metric, not an opinion.

02

Built to be trusted

An AI system is only useful if the people around it understand and believe it. I design the explanation alongside the system - confidence calibrated to data quality, red flags surfaced, decisions traceable.

03

Sequential, end-to-end

I work section by section, page by page, system by system - real constraints, real data, real outputs. No slideware. Everything I show has actually run.

04

Fluent in both worlds

I move between the technical and the business layer without losing either. That's where AI implementation usually breaks - and where I'm most useful.
